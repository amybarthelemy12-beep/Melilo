# Melilo

Apache 2.0 language model that turns court opinions, statutes, bills,
regulations, and declassified government documents into structured legal
analysis in plain English.

## Disclaimer

**Melilo is not a lawyer.** Outputs are educational explanations of legal
text. They are not legal advice and do not create an attorney-client
relationship. Do not rely on Melilo's output for any legal decision. For
matters with legal consequences, consult a licensed attorney in the
relevant jurisdiction. Melilo is not a substitute for reading the
underlying document.

## Intended users

- **Nonpartisan civic-information platforms** that explain what a law
  says, what a court decided, what a bill would do, or what a
  declassified document contains, to a general audience, without taking a
  side.
- **Law students** using PIRAC outputs and case briefs as study aids
  alongside the primary source.

## Tasks

Melilo is a single multi-task model. The task is selected by the prompt
(via `melilo.translate.prompts.build_messages`):

- **`pirac`** *(cases only)* — FIRAC-style analysis with sections
  `Facts/Parties`, `Issue`, `Rule`, `Analysis`, `Conclusion`.
- **`brief`** *(cases only)* — full case brief with sections `Citation`,
  `Facts`, `Procedural History`, `Issue`, `Holding`, `Reasoning`,
  `Disposition`.
- **`summary`** *(any source type)* — prose summary tailored to the
  source:
  - **Cases**: 2–4 sentences covering parties, decision, main reason.
  - **Statutes / bills / regulations**: prose lede plus a `## Key
    Provisions` bulleted list. Each bullet starts with the source's
    section identifier in backticks and a one-line plain-English
    description.
  - **Declassified**: 3–5 sentences of prose; redaction markers
    (`[REDACTED]`, `█████`, classification markings) are preserved
    verbatim where the redacted material would have been mentioned. No
    speculation about the contents of redactions.
- **`section_walkthrough`** *(statutes, bills, regulations,
  declassified)* — one pair record per § section. The chunker splits
  on lines beginning with `§`, `Sec.`, `SEC.`, `Section`, or `SECTION`,
  and the output starts with an H2 heading that reproduces the section
  identifier exactly as in the source.

When a required section cannot be supported by the source, the model
emits the literal string `Not stated in source.` rather than inventing
content.

Sponsors, status, fiscal impact, and similar metadata for bills are not
LLM-generated — those live as structured fields in the source database
and are joined to Melilo's output downstream.

## Model details

- **Base model**: `allenai/OLMo-2-0425-1B-SFT` (Apache 2.0). We start from the
  instruction-tuned variant rather than the raw pretrained checkpoint, so the
  chat template (Tülu 3) is inherited.
- **Fine-tuning method**: multi-task supervised fine-tuning across the
  four task types above. The task is identified entirely by the prompt;
  there are no task-id heads or embeddings.
- **Pair generator**: `allenai/Olmo-3-7B-Instruct` (Apache 2.0)
- **License**: Apache 2.0
- **Languages**: English

## Nonpartisan-by-design constraints

The system prompt used for every task imposes hard rules:

1. No praise, criticism, or characterization of parties, judges,
   lawmakers, agencies, or policy positions.
2. No legal advice or predictions about outcomes.
3. Only facts present in the source may be used.
4. Citations, section numbers, defined terms, and redaction markers are
   preserved exactly.
5. Legal terms with no plain equivalent are restated in everyday words,
   with the original term kept in parentheses.

These constraints are part of the SFT training data, so they survive
distillation into the student.

## Training data

Pure distillation. Pairs are generated by prompting
`Olmo-3-7B-Instruct` over raw historical documents in a Cloudflare R2
bucket. There are no hand-labeled examples in v1; every output in the
training set comes from the translator. Each pair record stores the task
type, source type, section identifier (when applicable), source URI,
source text, structured output, translator model id, prompt version, and
timestamp.

Recommended public corpora (all Apache-2.0 or public-domain compatible):
CourtListener / Free Law Project, the Caselaw Access Project,
govinfo.gov for federal statutes and bills, Congress.gov for current
bills, and FOIA reading rooms / the National Security Archive for
declassified material.

## Out of scope

- Producing legal advice, predictions about case outcomes, or
  jurisdiction-specific guidance.
- Editorializing about parties, judges, lawmakers, agencies, or policy.
- Translating between human languages.
- Contracts, terms of service, and other non-court / non-statute /
  non-regulation / non-government documents. These may be added in a
  later version but are not validated for v1.
- Use as the sole basis for any decision with legal or financial
  consequences.

## Limitations and known risks

- **Hallucination of legal effect**: the model may rephrase a term in a
  way that subtly changes its legal meaning. Treat all output as a draft
  to be checked against the source.
- **Citation drift**: the model is instructed to preserve citations
  verbatim, but distillation artifacts can introduce errors. Citations
  should always be verified.
- **Jurisdictional gaps**: legal terms have different meanings across
  jurisdictions; the model does not reliably distinguish them.
- **Source quality**: errors in OCR or PDF extraction propagate into
  outputs. For declassified docs this is especially severe — many are
  scanned at low quality.
- **Section detection**: the `section_walkthrough` chunker matches
  common header patterns (`§`, `Sec.`, `SECTION`, etc.) but will
  under-split documents that use idiosyncratic formatting. When no
  headers are detected, the whole document falls back to a single
  walkthrough record.
- **Teacher errors propagate**: with no human labels, every error the
  7B teacher makes can become a training signal. A held-out human
  review set is required before claiming production quality.
- **Distillation artifacts**: as a 1B student of a 7B teacher, Melilo
  will produce shorter, less nuanced analyses than the teacher in edge
  cases.
- **Neutrality is a constraint, not a guarantee**: the system prompt
  and training data push toward neutral output, but reviewers on civic
  platforms should still spot-check.

## Evaluation

To be added once v1 pairs are accumulated. Planned:

- **Structural completeness** — does each PIRAC / brief / walkthrough
  contain every required section, or the `Not stated in source.` token?
- **Faithfulness** — does the rewrite contain every obligation,
  exception, citation, and section number from the source, and no
  others?
- **Provisions coverage** — for `summary` on statutes/bills/regulations,
  does the Key Provisions list cover every enumerated section in the
  source?
- **Readability** — grade-level scores on prose output.
- **Neutrality audit** — held-out set of politically charged opinions,
  bills, and declassified documents reviewed for editorial language.
- **Redaction preservation** — declassified outputs checked against the
  source for verbatim retention of redaction markers.
- **Human review** on a held-out set, split between civic-platform
  reviewers and law-student readers.
